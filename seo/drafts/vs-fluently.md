<p>Fluently is an English coach for professionals who already speak and want to sound better at work, scoring each conversation on pronunciation, fluency, vocabulary and grammar. Vocele is a coach for adults who understand a language but freeze when they speak it, in nine languages, with pronunciation scored word by word and corrections drawn inside the sentence you said.</p>

<p>I studied Fluently from its listings and reviews while building Vocele, then audited my "what they have that we don't" list against our own code. Five of the six gaps I'd written down were already shipped or stale. That exercise taught me to read competitor marketing, including ours, with some suspicion, and it shaped how this page is written: from what each app's store page and reviewers actually say.</p>

<p>The shortest version of the contrast is price and audience. Fluently is $24.99 a month for English, aimed at professionals working toward C1. Vocele starts at $14.99 a month across nine languages, aimed at the adult who understands far more than they can say.</p>

<h2>What Fluently does well</h2>

<p>Fluently's loop is a voice conversation with a personal AI tutor, followed by a score across four axes: pronunciation, fluency, vocabulary and grammar. Its site frames the learning as "speaking, not endless grammar drills," and a user review on the homepage praises that the tutor "remembers all the past conversations." The scenario library leans professional: "Practice any situation. Your job, your industry," presentations, negotiations ("Hold Your Ground and Close Deals"), standups and all-hands. The company says it serves over 25,000 non-native professionals, and its plan copy talks about reaching C1 in 32 weeks.</p>

<p>The feature that separates it from Vocele is feedback on your real calls. Its Product Hunt listing describes an English coach that "helps you speak better on work calls" and can listen in on Zoom or Google Meet and give feedback afterwards. If your English problem lives in Tuesday's client call, that's the closest thing to a tutor sitting in on it. In late 2025 Fluently also shipped a free 30-second accent test, "Accent Guru," which is a smart way in.</p>

<p>Pricing is above Vocele's Core plan. The App Store lists a monthly plan at $24.99, three months at $44.99 to $89.99, annual at $89.99 to $179.99, and weekly options from $4.99. A separate "Workplace English Course" is $19.99. There's a free assessment, and the app holds 4.8 stars across about 5,500 ratings. The App Store listing now names five languages (English, French, Japanese, Portuguese and Spanish), though the company's site and every review I read treat it as an English product.</p>

<h2>Where Fluently falls short</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>One tutor, one voice.</strong> Reviewers note a single AI persona with no personality choice, and unclear support for British English.</li>
  <li><strong>The paywall arrives right after the assessment.</strong> Trustpilot reviews and the practiceme.app comparison mention it, alongside crashes and login problems.</li>
  <li><strong>Its most distinctive feature costs extra.</strong> The call analysis is a separate purchase on top of the subscription, per reviews.</li>
  <li><strong>It assumes you can already talk.</strong> The scenarios and the C1 framing are aimed at someone who holds conversations today and wants them sharper. If you understand everything and produce almost nothing, a four-axis report card mostly tells you what you already know.</li>
  <li><strong>Cancellation is reported as difficult.</strong></li>
</ul>

<h2>What Vocele does differently</h2>

<p>Start with the minuses. Vocele has no call analysis and won't grade your Zoom recording. It has no professional scenario catalogue. It's iPhone only. Those are real gaps if you're Fluently's target user. Now the other side.</p>

<h3>Built for people who freeze</h3>

<p>Vocele starts from the person who has a parent-teacher conference on Thursday and has dreaded it for a week. The coaches, Luna and Lorenzo, run in three modes: full scaffolding for when you need help building the sentence, corrections-only for when you just want to be fixed, and immersive for when you want no interruptions. Conversations are open-topic, so you rehearse the conversation you're actually facing, and the next day's plan is built from what you got wrong.</p>

<h3>Corrections you can see</h3>

<p>Fluently tells you how you scored. Vocele highlights the wrong words inside the bubble you just spoke, with the fix beneath. I chose that over a separate report because a report tells you what went wrong and the highlight tells you where, and where is what sticks.</p>

<h3>Pronunciation with a history</h3>

<p>Both apps score pronunciation. The difference is granularity and time. Vocele scores every word, names the sound that dragged the score down, and charts that sound over weeks. Fluently's pronunciation axis is one number per conversation. I'd argue the trend matters more than either number; the <a href="/blog/ai-accent-training">accent training explainer</a> covers why.</p>

<h3>Nine languages, explained in yours</h3>

<p>Vocele teaches Spanish, English, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Russian and Polish. Grammar rules are explained in the language you already speak (the interface runs in English, Spanish, Portuguese, Russian and Polish), and vocabulary returns on a spaced-repetition schedule across six practice modes.</p>

<h3>Games, with rules</h3>

<p>Fluently added games ("Practice through play. Compete, score, improve"). Vocele has streaks, friends-only duels and leaderboards too. What we did differently is the rules around them: we built confetti bursts and XP count-ups, then killed them as off-brand. There are no guilt notifications, no strangers on your leaderboard, and a missed day doesn't reset anything.</p>

<h2>Which one should you pick</h2>

<p><strong>Fluently</strong> is the better tool if English is your only target, you already hold conversations and want to sound sharper in meetings, and you'd pay for feedback on a real call.</p>

<p><strong>Vocele</strong> is the better tool if you understand far more than you can say, you're learning a language other than English, you want to see a specific sound improve in numbers, or the conversation you're dreading is with a doctor, a landlord or your kid's teacher rather than a client.</p>

<p>If what you want is a big guided lesson library at a low price, neither is your answer; I've compared Praktika separately in <a href="/vs/praktika">Vocele vs Praktika</a>. And if you're weighing any app against a human tutor at $200 to $320 a month, <a href="/blog/ai-speaking-coach-vs-human-tutor">the AI coach vs human tutor comparison</a> is honest about where the apps lose.</p>
