<p>Fluently is a paid AI speaking tutor that started as an English coach for work calls. It's $24.99 a month or $89.99 a year and up on the US App Store, rated 4.8. Only the opening assessment is free, and reviewers report a weekly cap its FAQ doesn't mention. It suits professionals who already talk; Vocele suits people who freeze.</p>

  <p>That's the short version of this Fluently app review, checked on 2 November 2026 against its App Store and Google Play listings, its own site, Trustpilot and Product Hunt. Below: what it costs, what users like and complain about, what Fluently does better than Vocele (the app I build), and where Vocele is the better fit.</p>

  <p>I first studied Fluently in August 2026, from its listings and reviews, and then audited my "what they have that we don't" list against our own code. Five of the six gaps I'd written down were already shipped or stale. Re-checking for this update, I found Fluently had moved too: new languages, a teams product, and its call feature gone from its own pages. Competitor pages go stale fast, ours included, so every claim below names its source.</p>

  <h2>Fluently price: is it free or paid?</h2>

  <p>Paid. Fluently says so itself in its replies to Google Play reviews: "We offer a free initial language assessment, while full conversation practice requires a subscription. Trial availability may vary by location and offer." Its site also has free extras that don't need an account, such as a mock-interview warm-up and an accent test called Accent Guru, launched in November 2025.</p>

  <p>The <a href="https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fluently-ai-language-learning/id6683289805">US App Store listing</a> shows these in-app purchases:</p>

  <ul>
    <li><strong>Monthly:</strong> $24.99</li>
    <li><strong>3 months:</strong> $44.99 or $89.99</li>
    <li><strong>Annual:</strong> $89.99 or $179.99</li>
    <li><strong>Weekly:</strong> $6.99, $9.99 or $19.99</li>
    <li><strong>Workplace English course:</strong> $19.99, sold separately</li>
  </ul>

  <p>The site has no public pricing page; reviewers say prices show up on a paywall after sign-up, and they vary by country and offer. Its FAQ pitches the app as "a human tutor, but available 24/7 and 20x cheaper."</p>

  <h2>Fluently app review: what users say</h2>

  <p>The ratings are high everywhere I looked: 4.8 from 6,207 ratings on the App Store, 4.7 from 44.9K reviews and more than a million downloads on <a href="https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.getfluently.app">Google Play</a>, and 4.8 from 2,616 reviews on <a href="https://www.trustpilot.com/review/getfluently.app">Trustpilot</a>, where 92% are five stars. People like talking to a tutor "just like a phone call", one that "remembers all the past conversations", as a reviewer quoted on its homepage puts it.</p>

  <p>The small one-star share is worth reading, because it repeats. On Trustpilot the themes are charges and renewals people didn't expect, refunds refused, a paywall that appears after a long sign-up, and trouble finding where to cancel. Two reviewers in August and September 2026 reported a new weekly time limit, one writing "It was not mentioned anywhere that it was only for 100 minutes per week". That sits awkwardly next to Fluently's own FAQ, which says Pro subscribers face "no daily or monthly limits". On Google Play, an August 2026 reviewer wrote that you have to pay "AT LEAST $80+ before you can even try the app".</p>

  <p>I take billing complaints seriously because I've been on the other side of them. Apple rejected Vocele three times under its subscription-disclosure rule before launch. I ended up writing a checker that fails our build when a paywall screen can't name the price a trial converts into, and it caught a "Start 3 days free" screen doing exactly that. So my reading of Fluently's one-star reviews is practical: before the trial, find the plan it converts into and the date.</p>

  <h2>What Fluently does well</h2>

  <p>Fluently's loop is a voice conversation with an AI tutor, scored across pronunciation, fluency, vocabulary and grammar; its About page adds comprehension as a fifth. The homepage leans into work English: team meetings, presentations, negotiations ("Hold Your Ground and Close Deals"), interview practice, daily AI calls and "sound-by-sound feedback". If your problem is sounding sharper at work, it's aimed squarely at you, and a new Fluently for Teams product sells the same coach to employers.</p>

  <p>It also covers more languages than it used to. Its <a href="https://getfluently.app/about">About page</a> says "Fluently teaches 15 languages": English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Polish, Russian, Korean, Hebrew, Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, Dutch and Greek. The homepage is still English-first, and every review I read treats it as an English product, but its store title is "AI Language Learning".</p>

  <p>Real-call feedback is where Fluently started. It began in 2023, per the same About page, as "a desktop AI speaking coach that listens to your side of real work calls". Its <a href="https://www.producthunt.com/products/fluently-ai-english-coach">Product Hunt listing</a> still calls it a coach "that helps you speak better on work calls". But I couldn't find the call feature on its current homepage or store listings, so I can't tell you whether you can buy it today.</p>

  <h2>Where Fluently falls short</h2>

  <ul>
    <li><strong>No way to try the conversation for free.</strong> The assessment is free; practice isn't. Fluently confirms this in its own Play replies.</li>
    <li><strong>Limits that aren't where you'd look.</strong> The FAQ promises no daily or monthly limits; Trustpilot reviewers report a weekly cap they found out about by hitting it.</li>
    <li><strong>Billing friction.</strong> Unexpected renewals and hard-to-find cancellation are the most repeated one-star themes, and Trustpilot's own summary mentions the cancellation option being hard to locate in the app.</li>
    <li><strong>One tutor style.</strong> PracticeMe, which makes a competing speaking app, says there's "no option to pick between personalities, genders, or styles". I couldn't confirm this on Fluently's pages.</li>
    <li><strong>It assumes you can already talk.</strong> Team meetings and negotiations are aimed at someone who holds conversations today and wants them sharper. If you understand everything and produce almost nothing, a four-axis report card mostly tells you what you already know.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>What Vocele does differently</h2>

  <p>Start with the minuses. Vocele doesn't listen to or grade your real calls, and it has no professional scenario catalogue. It's iPhone only, and it isn't on the App Store yet. Those are real gaps if you're Fluently's target user.</p>

  <h3>Built for people who freeze</h3>

  <p>Vocele starts from the person who has a parent-teacher conference on Thursday and has dreaded it for a week. The coaches, Luna and Lorenzo, run in three modes: full scaffolding when you need help building the sentence, corrections-only when you just want to be fixed, and immersive when you want no interruptions. Conversations are open-topic, so you rehearse the conversation you're actually facing, and the next day's plan is built from what you got wrong.</p>

  <h3>Corrections you can see</h3>

  <p>Fluently tells you how you scored. Vocele highlights the wrong words inside the bubble you just spoke, with the fix beneath. I chose that over a separate report because a report tells you what went wrong and the highlight tells you where, and where is what sticks.</p>

  <h3>Pronunciation with a history</h3>

  <p>Both apps go below the word: Fluently's homepage lists sound-by-sound feedback, and Vocele scores every word and names the sound that dragged it down. The difference I'd point to is time. Vocele charts each sound over weeks, and I couldn't find anything on Fluently's site or listing about tracking a sound over time. I'd argue the trend matters more than any single score; the <a href="/blog/ai-accent-training">accent training explainer</a> covers why.</p>

  <h3>Vocele shows its minutes before you pay</h3>

  <p>Vocele meters spoken conversation and pronunciation scoring on every plan, and says how much before you pay. Core is $14.99 a month with 150 conversation minutes and 30 pronunciation minutes; Fluency ($29.99) and Meta ($49.99) double and quadruple that. Word sets, stories, reviews and grammar don't use minutes. The numbers are on the <a href="/pricing">pricing page</a> before you subscribe.</p>

  <h3>Saved words return on a schedule</h3>

  <p>Vocele teaches Spanish, English, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Russian and Polish, with grammar rules explained in your native language when the app's interface supports it (English, Spanish, Portuguese, Russian or Polish). Vocabulary returns on a spaced-repetition schedule across six practice modes, and any phrase from a chat can go into a phrasebook with its own reviews.</p>

  <h3>Games, with rules</h3>

  <p>Fluently has games ("Practice through play. Compete, score, improve"). Vocele has streaks, duels and friends-only leaderboards. We built confetti bursts and XP count-ups, then killed them as off-brand. There are no guilt notifications, no strangers on your leaderboard, and no punitive streak reset.</p>

  <h2>Which one should you pick</h2>

  <p><strong>Fluently</strong> is the better tool if you already hold conversations in English and want to sound sharper in meetings and interviews, you're on Android, or your employer is paying through its teams plan.</p>

  <p><strong>Vocele</strong> is the better tool if you understand far more than you can say, you want to see a specific sound improve in numbers, you want words to come back until they stick, or the conversation you're dreading is with a doctor, a landlord or your kid's teacher rather than a client.</p>
