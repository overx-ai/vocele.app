# Best AI Language Tutor App in 2026: 7 Compared by Problem

**Short answer:** no single AI language tutor app is best for everyone in 2026, so pick by what stops you speaking. Beginners who want a course do well with Speak, people who like a friendly face with Praktika, and anyone learning a rare language with TalkPal. Fluently targets work calls, ELSA Speak and BoldVoice target accent only, and Vocele marks corrections inside your own sentence.

Every roundup on page one of Google picks a different winner. Most are written by affiliates or by an app that ranks itself first. I'm one of those apps: I built Vocele, so read this with that in mind. I'll put us last and tell you where each of the others beats us.

When I picked the App Store name for Vocele, "AI language tutor" was already the exact title of four apps: Praktika, Lily Speak, BetterSpeak and Verbatica. I went with "coach" instead. When four apps share one name, the name tells you nothing about what the app is good at. So this list sorts the apps by the problem they solve.

## How to test an AI tutor in a ten-minute trial

Check the claims in the app, not on the website. When I researched Fluently, Praktika, Speak and ELSA, I wrote down every feature they had that we didn't, then checked each one against our own code. Five of the six "gaps" were already shipped or out of date. Feature lists written by marketing teams overstate the differences.

So here's the test I'd run in any free trial, in this order:

1. **Say one real thing about your week.** Skip the suggested lesson and tell it what you did on Saturday. Some apps are mostly tapping with a speaking exercise at the end, and some can't follow you off their script. You'll know in a minute.
2. **Make a mistake on purpose.** Say "I have 30 years" instead of "I'm 30", or "yesterday I go". A score at the end tells you something went wrong. A highlight on the wrong words tells you where.
3. **Say a word you know you mispronounce.** For a lot of learners that's anything with "th" or the short "i" of *ship*. See whether the app names the sound or just gives you a number, and whether it keeps a history. One score is a verdict. A trend over weeks is what tells you whether you're improving.
4. **Check your language and your phone.** Most apps below run on Android and the web, one is iPhone only, and language lists range from English only to 130+.
5. **Read the subscription screen.** Note whether the plan renews monthly, every three months or yearly. On iPhone, a subscription bought through the App Store is cancelled in Settings, under your name and then Subscriptions.

## 1. Speak: the structured course

Speak is the most course-like app here. You follow lessons built around real-life scenarios, speak your lines and get a score for pronunciation, intonation and fluency. It teaches Spanish, Korean, Japanese, Chinese, English, French and Italian, and runs on iOS, Android and the web. The higher tier, Premium Plus, adds lessons generated from your own mistakes.

Where it beats us is structure. If you don't want to decide what to practice, Speak decides for you. LanguaTalk, a rival tutoring site, says its course structure works well for beginners, and Headway, a rival app, says it removes decision fatigue.

It's a weaker fit for intermediate and advanced learners. Headway says they hit a ceiling, and LanguaTalk found the speech recognition overly lenient. The full side-by-side is in [Vocele vs Speak](/vs/speak).

## 2. Praktika: avatars and a big lesson library

Praktika puts an AI avatar in front of you that adapts to your tone, pace and goals. Its App Store listing advertises 1,000+ lessons, including IELTS and TOEFL prep, and you can upload photos, PDFs and voice notes to talk about. It's on iOS, Android and the web.

The lesson library and the avatar are what it does better than us. If talking to a face makes it easier, Praktika is built for that.

I'd skip it if you want to pay month to month, since LanguaTalk reports 3-month or annual plans only, or if you want spaced-repetition vocabulary review, which LanguaTalk says it doesn't have. The longer version is in [Vocele vs Praktika](/vs/praktika).

## 3. TalkPal: the most languages

TalkPal lists 130+ languages, more than anything else here. Its App Store listing names a mode for nearly every format: chat, words, sentences, dialogues, calls, role-plays, characters and debates. It runs on the web, iOS and Android.

Coverage is the reason to pick it. If you're learning a language none of the others teach, TalkPal may be the only app on this list that has it.

Advanced learners will outgrow it. LanguaTalk found the voices robotic, the sentences too simple at higher levels, and no dedicated grammar lessons. Full comparison: [Vocele vs TalkPal](/vs/talkpal).

## 4. Fluently: speaking practice for work

Fluently is aimed at work. Its site talks about standups, all-hands, presentations and closing deals, and it scores your pronunciation, fluency, vocabulary and grammar. Its Product Hunt listing says it can listen in on real Zoom or Google Meet calls and give feedback, a feature I didn't see advertised by the others.

That's feedback on the meetings you actually have, and if your problem is the Monday call, it's a real advantage over us.

One caution: its About page says 13 languages while one of its App Store storefronts lists five, so check in the app before you subscribe. More in [Vocele vs Fluently](/vs/fluently).

## 5. ELSA Speak: the pronunciation specialist

ELSA teaches English only, and it's built around pronunciation. Headway, a rival app, describes its feedback as instant and color-coded at the phoneme level, so you see which sound in which word went wrong. It has a bilingual AI tutor that explains in your native language, and it runs on iOS and Android.

**What it does better than us:** depth on English pronunciation. The whole app is built around that one skill.

If your problem is putting sentences together, look elsewhere: Headway notes it gives little help with grammar or vocabulary. Our side-by-side is [Vocele vs ELSA](/vs/elsa).

## 6. BoldVoice: American accent training

BoldVoice is accent training for the American accent, with lessons from dialect coaches who have worked with Hollywood actors. Video tutorials with mouth diagrams show you how each sound is made, and it analyzes your words sound by sound. It also has conversation practice and AI role-play, on iOS, Android and the web.

Where it clearly wins is showing *how* to make a sound, which a score alone can't do.

**Who it's not for:** people still learning English. The Educational App Store review says so directly: "This isn't really an app for someone just learning the English language." The details are in [Vocele vs BoldVoice](/vs/boldvoice).

## 7. Vocele: conversation with corrections inside your sentence

This is ours, so it goes last. Vocele is an AI language coach for iPhone. You talk, by voice or text, with named coaches (Luna or Lorenzo) who answer out loud, on whatever topic you bring, with no scripted lesson. When you get something wrong, the wrong words are highlighted inside your own sentence with the fix underneath, and you can save the corrected sentence.

I designed corrections that way on purpose. Rewriting your whole sentence in a separate card shows you *what* the right version is. Marking the exact words shows you *where* you broke it, which is what you need next time.

It also scores pronunciation word by word, down to the sound, and keeps your score history week by week. Words you save from a conversation, with a long-press, a coach chip or a correction, go into spaced-repetition review and then come back in a spoken turn with the coach. You can choose full support, corrections only or an immersive mode. It teaches nine languages: Spanish, English, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Russian and Polish.

**Who it's not for:** Android and web users (it's iPhone only, iOS 17+), anyone who wants a scripted course or a library of scenarios (we don't have one), and anyone learning a language outside those nine. It also isn't on the App Store yet.

## Which one should you pick?

- **You're a beginner and want to be told what to do:** Speak.
- **You freeze in front of people and a friendly face helps:** Praktika.
- **Your language isn't on anyone else's list:** TalkPal.
- **You already speak English and the problem is work meetings:** Fluently.
- **People understand your English but your accent bothers you:** ELSA Speak, or BoldVoice for an American accent. More on how scoring works in [AI accent training, explained](/blog/ai-accent-training).
- **You understand a lot but go quiet when it's your turn, and you want to see where your sentences break:** Vocele.

## FAQ

**Can an AI tutor replace a human tutor?**
For speaking practice, mostly yes: an AI tutor is there at 11 p.m., doesn't get tired of the same question and costs less. A human is still better at noticing why you're stuck and at keeping you accountable. My advice: practice with the app daily and see a tutor once in a while if you can afford both.

**Which AI tutor app is best for pronunciation?**
For English pronunciation alone, ELSA Speak or BoldVoice, because pronunciation is their whole focus. If you want pronunciation scoring inside a conversation app, Speak, Vocele and Fluently all score it. Vocele keeps your pronunciation score history over weeks.

**Which AI tutor app has the most languages?**
TalkPal, with 130+ languages listed on its site. Speak teaches seven, Vocele nine, and ELSA Speak and BoldVoice teach English only. Fluently's site and store listing disagree, so check its current list in the app.

**Are AI language tutor apps free?**
Free to download, paid to use fully. The App Store listings for Speak, Praktika, TalkPal, Fluently and ELSA Speak all show a free download with in-app purchases. Vocele isn't on the App Store yet; free accounts will be able to try it with limited access, and what's included is shown in the app.

**How do I know if an AI tutor is helping?**
Record yourself saying the same short answer on day one and again after a month. If the app scores pronunciation, compare this month's scores with last month's rather than reading one day's number.
