<p>TalkPal is the broader app: 130+ languages, eight conversation modes including roleplays and debates, a web version and a free daily allowance. Vocele is the deeper one in nine languages: vocabulary that returns on a spaced-repetition schedule, grammar explained in your own language, pronunciation diagnosed down to the sound and tracked over weeks, and corrections drawn inside the sentence you said.</p>

<p>In August I read Fluently, Praktika, Speak and ELSA from their listings and reviews, wrote down what they had that we didn't, then checked that list against our own code. Five of the six gaps were already shipped or stale. Since then I read competitor marketing, and ours, with the same suspicion, so everything below about TalkPal comes from its App Store listing, its site and three independent reviews, all linked from the facts they support.</p>

<h2>What TalkPal does well</h2>

<p>Variety is TalkPal's strength. The App Store listing names Chat, Word, Sentence, Dialogue and Call modes, plus Roleplays for real-life scenarios, Characters for conversations with famous figures, and Debates to push spontaneous speech. The site adds a Photo mode, grammar and video lessons. If you get bored easily, few apps give you more ways to start talking.</p>

<p>Its reach is the other headline. The listing's subtitle reads "Learn English, Spanish &amp; 130+," and it runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Vision Pro, Android and the web. It holds 4.7 stars across about 3,700 US ratings.</p>

<p>You can try it without paying. There's a free plan with a 10-minute daily limit and a 14-day Premium trial. The US App Store lists Premium at $19.99 for a month, $49.99 for three months and $119.99 for a year. LanguaTalk saw $59.88 a year in February 2026 and Lingrow reports $89.99 a year in some regions, so check yours.</p>

<p>One thing I'll concede outright: TalkPal already colors your pronunciation word by word. LanguaTalk's review calls the "color-coded word-level feedback" helpful. If an alternative page tells you word-level scoring is what TalkPal lacks, it's wrong.</p>

<h2>Where TalkPal falls short</h2>

<ul>
  <li><strong>The color doesn't tell you what to do.</strong> LanguaTalk rated it 3 out of 5 and found the pronunciation feedback short on "actionable guidance," with "no post-lesson feedback summary; only a generic daily recap."</li>
  <li><strong>Words don't come back.</strong> The same review found "no spaced repetition for vocabulary review." PracticeMe notes that words aren't saved automatically from your conversations.</li>
  <li><strong>Grammar is incidental.</strong> Per LanguaTalk, there are "no dedicated grammar lessons; grammar is only addressed incidentally." The site now lists grammar and video lessons, so this may be changing.</li>
  <li><strong>Breadth costs depth.</strong> LanguaTalk found sentences "too simple for higher levels," and Lingrow reports errors in some Japanese word decks and in how Chinese tones are displayed.</li>
  <li><strong>Billing complaints.</strong> Lingrow read the 50 most recent US reviews and found billing the most repeated complaint.</li>
</ul>

<p>The transcription point matters more than it sounds. Lingrow warns that "mistaken transcription can make the correction less useful": if the app hears a different word from the one you said, it corrects a sentence you never spoke. Any speaking app has this risk, including Vocele. Vocele's phrase drills sidestep it: you're scored against a phrase the app already knows, sound by sound, so there's no guessing what you meant.</p>

<h2>What Vocele does differently</h2>

<p>The minuses first. Vocele teaches nine languages, not 130. It has no roleplay catalogue, no famous characters, no debates and no web or Android app. It's iPhone only and not on the App Store yet. If you want a rare language or a laptop, TalkPal wins. Here's what you get instead.</p>

<h3>Pronunciation with a diagnosis and a history</h3>

<p>Vocele scores every word too, then names the sound that pulled the score down and charts that sound over weeks. You can drill the phrase and check any spoken chat turn on demand. It's the opinion I'd argue hardest: a single score is a verdict, and a trend is a coach. Seeing your score on the "th" sound climb week over week is what keeps you practicing. The <a href="/blog/ai-accent-training">accent training explainer</a> goes into how the scoring works.</p>

<h3>Corrections inside your own sentence</h3>

<p>When you say something wrong, Vocele highlights the wrong words in the bubble you just spoke and puts the fix beneath, with a rule chip you can open. I chose that over rewriting the whole sentence in a separate card because a rewrite shows you what's right, and the highlight shows you where you went wrong. One tap saves the corrected sentence to your phrasebook.</p>

<h3>Words that come back</h3>

<p>Long-press any word in chat, or save a correction, and it joins your vocabulary. It then returns on a spaced-repetition schedule in six modes: flashcards, matching, typing, listening, pictures and fill-in-the-blank. Stories do the same work from the other side, with scenes tuned to your level and questions you answer aloud.</p>

<h3>Grammar in the language you already speak</h3>

<p>Rules are explained in your native language (the interface runs in English, Spanish, Portuguese, Russian and Polish), with adaptive quizzes and a report of the sentences where you could have used the rule. The coaches, Luna and Lorenzo, run in three modes: full scaffolding when you need help building the sentence, corrections-only, and immersive. Tomorrow's plan is built from today's mistakes.</p>

<h2>Which one should you pick</h2>

<p><strong>TalkPal</strong> is the better choice if your language isn't one of Vocele's nine, you want to practice on Android or a laptop, you like switching between roleplays, debates and characters, or you want to talk a little every day for free.</p>

<p><strong>Vocele</strong> is the better choice if you understand far more than you can say and <a href="/blog/understand-english-but-cant-speak">freeze when it's your turn</a>, you want the words from today's conversation to still be there next month, or you want to see a specific sound improve in numbers rather than colors.</p>
