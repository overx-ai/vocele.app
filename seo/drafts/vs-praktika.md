<p>Praktika has more languages, avatars and a lower price. Vocele has the learning system around the conversation that Praktika lacks: spaced-repetition vocabulary, stories you answer aloud, grammar explained in your own language, pronunciation scored word by word over weeks, and corrections drawn inside the sentence you said. Breadth and a face: Praktika. Words that stick and a measurable accent: Vocele.</p>

<p>Before designing Vocele I collected 46 screenshots of Praktika's onboarding, chat and paywall as design references, and I read the two long reviews cited here. I came away thinking its avatar conversations are the most convincing in the category, so this is the comparison I wanted when I was deciding whether to build ours at all, written without a grudge.</p>

<h2>What Praktika does well</h2>

<p>Praktika's core loop is a spoken conversation with an animated AI avatar. Its App Store listing describes avatars "that adapt to your tone, pace, and goals." For a beginner, talking to a face lowers the barrier in a way a chat bubble doesn't.</p>

<p>The library is enormous. The listing counts over a thousand lessons across academic prep (IELTS, TOEFL), lifestyle, travel, career paths and slang, plus a multimodal feature that builds lessons from a photo, a PDF or a voice note you upload. Twelve languages: English, Spanish, Japanese, Korean, French, German, Italian, European and Brazilian Portuguese, Russian, Chinese and Arabic. It runs on iOS, Android and the web.</p>

<p>And it's cheap. Praktika's own site says roughly $8 a month on the annual plan, against its estimate of $400 a month for a private tutor. The App Store lists a 1-month option at $9.99, 3 months at $49.99 and annual plans at $119.99 to $139.99, though two independent reviews report that inside the app only the 3-month and annual plans are offered. There's a free tier with limits and a 7-day trial that asks for payment details up front.</p>

<h2>Where Praktika falls short</h2>

<p>The complaints in the reviews I read are consistent with each other, which is why I trust them.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Feedback is buried.</strong> Corrections live inside the chat flow, and there's no end-of-lesson summary of what you got wrong. You scroll back to find your mistakes.</li>
  <li><strong>The learning path is rigid.</strong> Change your goal and your progress resets. Conversations follow a script, and wandering off-topic gets pulled back.</li>
  <li><strong>Vocabulary doesn't come back.</strong> Words from a lesson aren't scheduled for spaced-repetition review, so they fade.</li>
  <li><strong>Pronunciation gets flagged, then forgotten.</strong> Praktika upgraded its pronunciation feedback in 2025, and one review notes inconsistent handling of British pronunciation. Neither review describes a way to see whether a specific sound is improving month over month.</li>
  <li><strong>Refunds are hard</strong>, and there's no month-to-month plan in practice.</li>
</ul>

<p>None of these matter much if what you want is a guided course. They matter a lot if what you want is to fix one accent problem and prove to yourself it's fixed.</p>

<h2>What Vocele does differently</h2>

<p>The honest minuses first. Vocele has no avatars; its coaches, Luna and Lorenzo, are voices with names. It teaches seven languages: Spanish, English, French, German, Italian, Portuguese and Japanese. It's iPhone only. And it costs more, starting at $14.99 a month. What you get for that is a complete learning system, with the conversation at its centre.</p>

<h3>Corrections inside your own sentence</h3>

<p>When you say "I have five years experience in marketing," Vocele highlights the wrong words in the bubble you just spoke and puts the fix beneath, instead of replying with a corrected paragraph: "I've been working in marketing for five years." I chose that over a separate correction card because a card tells you the right sentence and the highlight tells you where you broke yours. Where is what you remember. There's also a corrections-only mode that stops the coach explaining and just fixes.</p>

<h3>Per-word pronunciation scores, with history</h3>

<p>Every word you say in accent practice gets a score. Something like: marketing 96, years 91, experience 78. Then it names the sound in "experience" that dragged the number down. I'll admit the number is the less important half. What I care about is the chart of that same sound, scored every week, going up. A single score is a verdict. A trend is a coach. The <a href="/blog/ai-accent-training">accent training explainer</a> covers how the scoring works.</p>

<h3>A word-learning system, which Praktika doesn't have</h3>

<p>This is the gap the reviews keep naming on Praktika's side, and it's where Vocele is strongest. Vocabulary runs on spaced repetition, so a word from Tuesday's conversation returns on Friday, before you'd forget it, across six practice modes: flashcards, matching, typing, listening, picture and cloze rounds. Themed word sets cover the situations you're actually in, from work and finance to the doctor and your kid's school. Any phrase from a chat, including a correction, can be saved into a phrasebook with its own review schedule. Grammar rules are explained in your own language (the interface runs in English, Spanish, Portuguese, Russian and Polish), and after a conversation you get a report of the sentences where you could have used the rule you just practised.</p>

<h3>Stories you answer out loud</h3>

<p>Reading isn't a separate silent module. Short scenes are generated at your CEFR level, read aloud to you, and then you answer comprehension questions by speaking. You retell the same scene from another character's point of view, and the story comes back days later so it sticks. That's more material per week than a fixed lesson library, because it's generated at your level rather than picked from a shelf.</p>

<h3>A plan built from your errors</h3>

<p>You pick interests during onboarding, and each day's plan is built from what you got wrong yesterday, across conversation, accent, words, stories and grammar. Conversations are open-topic, so if the thing on your mind is Thursday's job interview, you talk about Thursday's job interview.</p>

<h2>Which one should you pick</h2>

<p><strong>Pick Praktika if</strong> you're a beginner who wants a guided path, you learn better talking to a face, you need Korean, Chinese, Arabic or Russian, or price decides it. It's a very good app for that.</p>

<p><strong>Pick Vocele if</strong> you already understand a lot and your problem is output: you freeze, or people ask you to repeat yourself. If you want vocabulary that actually stays learned instead of fading after the lesson. If you want to see, in numbers, that your accent is changing. And if you're on iPhone.</p>

<p><strong>Pick neither if</strong> you need a human. Both apps cost far less than a private tutor at $200 to $320 a month for two lessons a week, and I've written up <a href="/blog/ai-speaking-coach-vs-human-tutor">where an AI coach loses to a tutor</a>. Some people need the accountability of a person.</p>

<h2>A note on our own gaps</h2>

<p>When I audited what Praktika, Fluently, Speak and ELSA had that Vocele didn't, five of the six gaps on my list turned out to be already shipped in our own code. The lesson cut both ways: competitor marketing overstates gaps, including ours. Treat the table above as what each app does today, from its store listing and the reviews cited, and expect it to change.</p>
