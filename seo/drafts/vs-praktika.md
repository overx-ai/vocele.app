<p>Praktika is good for a beginner who wants a guided course and a tutor with a face. It holds 4.7 stars from about 167,000 App Store ratings and 3.8 on Trustpilot. It's weak at keeping the words you meet, and I found no way in it to track whether your accent is improving. Billing after the free trial is the top complaint.</p>

  <p>That's my Praktika app review in one paragraph, checked against its App Store listing, its own site, Trustpilot and LanguaTalk's review on 2 November 2026. The rest of this page is the detail behind it, including what Praktika does better than Vocele, the app I build.</p>

  <p>I've looked at Praktika more closely than most reviewers. While designing Vocele I collected 46 screenshots of Praktika's onboarding, chat and paywall as design references.</p>

  <p>When I named our App Store listing, I picked "Coach" over "Tutor" partly because "AI language tutor" is an exact-title collision between four apps, Praktika among them. I think its tutors are the most lifelike faces I've seen in an AI language app. This page is written without a grudge.</p>

  <h2>Praktika review: what users and reviewers say</h2>

  <p>The rating depends on where you look. The <a href="https://apps.apple.com/us/app/praktika-ai-language-tutor/id1624701477">US App Store listing</a> shows 4.7 stars from 167,545 ratings. <a href="https://www.trustpilot.com/review/praktika.ai">Trustpilot</a> shows 3.8 from 1,701 reviews, and that average hides a split: 49% of reviewers give five stars and 16% give one. <a href="https://languatalk.com/blog/praktika-review/">LanguaTalk's review</a>, last updated in June 2026, gives it 3 out of 5.</p>

  <p>The praise is consistent. Trustpilot reviewers in October 2026 liked getting instant feedback on pronunciation, a tutor that "does not get grumpy or frustrated if you get something wrong", and varied lessons. LanguaTalk found the conversations natural, especially at intermediate level, and the lesson paths well organised.</p>

  <p>So are the complaints. The most common one on Trustpilot is money: a plan that renewed for a year after the trial with no reminder, and refunds that were refused.</p>

  <p>After that come speech recognition that mishears you, a voice that hangs mid-lesson, lessons that circle back over old material, and personalisation that doesn't follow your progress. An App Store reviewer called the avatar tutors gimmicky because their lips don't sync with the speech, and wanted a way to hide the transcript without switching to full video-call mode.</p>

  <p>My verdict is that Praktika is a good guided conversation course at a low price. If keeping vocabulary or measuring your accent is why you're shopping, keep reading. If not, Praktika is probably the cheaper right answer.</p>

  <h2>What Praktika does well</h2>

  <p>Praktika's core loop is a spoken conversation with an AI tutor on screen. The listing says "Each tutor has a distinct personality and accent (American, British, Latin American, and more)", and that the tutor can explain things in the language you already speak. Its site adds a tone setting (Soft, Balanced or Strict). For a nervous beginner, talking to a face lowers the barrier in a way a chat bubble doesn't.</p>

  <p>The library is big. The listing counts "1,000+ lessons for travel, careers, job interviews, business, everyday conversation, slang, and IELTS and TOEFL prep", and you can upload a photo, a PDF, a video or a voice note and turn it into a lesson; its example is rehearsing interview answers from your own resume.</p>

  <p>It teaches thirteen languages: English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese (European and Brazilian), Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Arabic, Russian, Polish and Dutch. It runs on iPhone, Android and the web.</p>

  <p>It's also actively maintained. Version 4.42.0 shipped on 26 October 2026, four days after the previous release.</p>

  <h2>Praktika price in 2026, and what "$8 a month" means</h2>

  <p><a href="https://praktika.ai/">Praktika's homepage</a> says "~$8 / month", against "~$400 / month" for a private human tutor. The US App Store tells a more detailed story. Its in-app purchase list runs from $6.99 to $139.99 and includes a $9.99 one-month plan, $49.99 for three months, annual plans at $49.99, $119.99 and $139.99, and a $19.99 three-month promo. Prices vary by country and offer.</p>

  <p>Two things follow. The $8 figure only works out on an annual plan: $8 a month is $96 a year, between the $49.99 and $119.99 annual prices on the listing, and the one-month plan costs more. And older reviews are out of date on this point. LanguaTalk and PracticeMe both reported only 3-month and annual plans with no month-to-month option, but the $9.99 one-month plan is on the listing today. If the trial-renewal complaints worry you, start with the shortest plan and set a reminder before the trial ends.</p>

  <h2>Where Praktika falls short</h2>

  <ul>
    <li><strong>Saved words don't come back.</strong> LanguaTalk found "No spaced repetition or long-term tracking of saved vocabulary". You meet a word in a lesson and that's the last time the app asks you about it.</li>
    <li><strong>No end-of-lesson summary.</strong> The listing promises you'll "know exactly what to improve after every conversation", but LanguaTalk found "no end-of-lesson feedback summary". Corrections sit behind a button on each line, so you scroll back to find them.</li>
    <li><strong>Rigid paths.</strong> Per LanguaTalk, changing your learning path resets your progress. PracticeMe, which makes a competing speaking app, adds that conversations follow a script and wandering off-topic gets pulled back to the lesson.</li>
    <li><strong>No per-sound history that I could find.</strong> Praktika gives pronunciation feedback in lessons. Neither the listing nor the reviews describe a way to see whether one specific sound is improving month over month.</li>
    <li><strong>Trial billing.</strong> The single most repeated Trustpilot complaint. Read the plan the trial converts into before you start it.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>None of this matters much if you want a guided course. It matters a lot if you want to fix one accent problem and prove to yourself it's fixed, or if you're tired of learning the same word three times.</p>

  <h2>What Vocele does differently</h2>

  <p>The honest minuses first. Vocele has no avatars; its coaches, Luna and Lorenzo, are voices with names. It teaches nine languages: Spanish, English, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Russian and Polish. It's iPhone only.</p>

  <p>It also costs more, starting at $14.99 a month, and spoken conversation and pronunciation scoring are metered on every plan (Core includes 150 conversation minutes and 30 pronunciation minutes a month; the <a href="/pricing">pricing page</a> lists the rest). What you get for that is review and tracking around the conversation.</p>

  <h3>Corrections inside your own sentence</h3>

  <p>When you say "I have five years experience in marketing," Vocele highlights the gap in the bubble you just spoke and puts the fix beneath it: "five years <strong>of</strong> experience." I chose that over a separate correction card because a card tells you the right sentence and the highlight tells you where you broke yours. The where is the part you remember. It's also the answer to Praktika's missing summary: the correction stays in the sentence you said, and you can save the corrected version to your phrasebook. There's a corrections-only mode for when you just want to be fixed, and a post-session analysis at the end.</p>

  <h3>Per-word pronunciation scores, with history</h3>

  <p>Every word you say in accent practice gets a score, and Vocele names the sound that dragged a low one down. I'll admit the number is the less important half. A single score is a verdict; a trend is a coach. What I care about is a sound's history going up week after week. The <a href="/blog/ai-accent-training">accent training explainer</a> covers how the scoring works, and if an accent course is what you're really after, <a href="/vs/boldvoice">Vocele vs BoldVoice</a> compares the two approaches.</p>

  <h3>A word-learning system, which Praktika doesn't have</h3>

  <p>This is the gap LanguaTalk names on Praktika's side, and it's where Vocele is strongest. Vocabulary runs on spaced repetition, so a word from a conversation comes back before you'd forget it, across six practice modes: flashcards, matching, typing, listening, picture and cloze rounds.</p>

  <p>Themed word sets cover work, travel, the doctor and school. Any phrase from a chat can go into a phrasebook with its own review schedule. Grammar rules are explained in your own language, with reports of sentences where you could have used each one.</p>

  <h3>Stories you answer out loud</h3>

  <p>Short scenes are tuned to your CEFR level and read aloud to you, and then you answer comprehension questions by speaking. You retell the scene from another character's point of view, and the story comes back days later.</p>

  <h3>A plan built from your errors</h3>

  <p>Each day's plan is built from yesterday's errors, and conversations are open-topic. If the thing on your mind is Thursday's job interview, you talk about Thursday's job interview instead of finishing lesson 14.</p>

  <h2>Which one should you pick</h2>

  <p><strong>Pick Praktika if</strong> you're a beginner who wants a guided path, you learn better talking to a face, you need Korean, Chinese, Arabic or Dutch, you're on Android, or price decides it. It's a very good app for that.</p>

  <p><strong>Pick Vocele if</strong> you already understand a lot and your problem is output: you freeze, or people ask you to repeat yourself. It also fits if you want vocabulary that stays learned after the lesson, or want to see in numbers that your accent is changing, and you're on iPhone.</p>

  <p><strong>Pick neither if</strong> you need a human. Both apps cost far less than a private tutor at $200 to $320 a month for two lessons a week, and I've written up <a href="/blog/ai-speaking-coach-vs-human-tutor">where an AI coach loses to a tutor</a>. If your language isn't on either list, TalkPal covers more than 130; <a href="/vs/talkpal">Vocele vs TalkPal</a> compares the two.</p>
