  <p>Speak is the more polished guided course: structured lessons that suit beginners, smooth voices, apps on iPhone and Android, and Korean and Chinese, which Vocele doesn't teach. Vocele is the alternative once the course stops stretching you: open conversation in three support modes, corrections drawn inside your own sentence, words that come back on a schedule, and German, Portuguese, Russian and Polish, which Speak doesn't list.</p>

  <p>If you're a beginner who wants someone to decide today's lesson, Speak is a good pick. People search for a Speak alternative for three reasons that come up again and again in reviews: they've hit the ceiling at intermediate level, the feedback feels thin, or the language they want isn't on Speak's list. I've also compared Vocele with <a href="/vs/praktika">Praktika</a>, <a href="/vs/talkpal">TalkPal</a>, <a href="/vs/fluently">Fluently</a> and <a href="/vs/elsa">ELSA Speak</a>.</p>

  <p>I build Vocele, so treat this page as a biased source. In August I read Fluently, Praktika, Speak and ELSA from their listings and reviews and wrote down what they had that we didn't. When I checked that list against our own code, five of the six gaps were already shipped or stale. The lesson I took: competitor lists built from marketing copy overstate gaps. Everything below about Speak comes from its App Store listing, its own site and two reviews, and both reviews come from companies that sell something else. I name them where I quote them.</p>

  <h2>What Speak does well</h2>

  <p>Speak has 4.8 stars from about 65,000 ratings on the US App Store, and its site cites more than 15 million downloads. LanguaTalk, which sells its own tutoring app, gave it 3 out of 5 in a review last updated in February 2026 and still called it "one of the more polished AI-based speaking apps available," with voices that are "smooth and easy to understand." Headway, another rival, writes that its "structured curriculum removes decision fatigue."</p>

  <p>For a beginner, that structure is most of the value. LanguaTalk says "the course structure works well for beginners." You open the app, it tells you what to do, and you repeat useful phrases until they come out without thinking. Headway also notes that "words are highlighted as you say them so you can see exactly where the AI flags a problem," which is close to how Vocele marks errors, so I won't pretend that part is unique to us.</p>

  <p>Speak runs on iPhone, Android and the web. On its site, Premium Plus adds lessons generated from your mistakes and a review built from your weak spots. If you live on Android, that alone settles it: Vocele is iPhone only.</p>

  <h2>Where Speak falls short</h2>

  <ul>
    <li><strong>The ceiling at intermediate.</strong> Headway lists "intermediate and advanced learners hit a ceiling" as a con. LanguaTalk found that Tutor Lessons and Vocab Builders are "almost completely absent from the intermediate and advanced levels."</li>
    <li><strong>Lenient scoring.</strong> LanguaTalk calls the speech recognition "overly lenient" and says that in Speaking Drills you can "reverse the word order entirely and still receive a perfect score." Headway agrees that it "can be overly lenient," while calling it "among the most accurate available in consumer language apps."</li>
    <li><strong>Thin feedback.</strong> LanguaTalk: "Feedback is brief and lacks depth or personalization."</li>
    <li><strong>Vocabulary review depends on who you ask.</strong> LanguaTalk found "no spaced repetition or long-term vocabulary tracking." Headway describes a Smart Review that "resurfaces weak words and phrases over time," and Speak's site puts the personalized review in Premium Plus. Check which plan you're on before counting on it.</li>
    <li><strong>No voice or accent choice.</strong> Headway says English "defaults to American English with no option to switch to British English," and LanguaTalk found no choice of voices or regional accents.</li>
    <li><strong>No lasting free tier.</strong> According to Headway, "there is no ongoing free tier that supports daily practice"; the free access is a 7-day trial.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>Speak sells two paid tiers, Premium and Premium Plus, and LanguaTalk notes the difference isn't always clear at signup. Prices vary by country and promotion, so read the plan in the store before the trial converts.</p>

  <h2>What Vocele does differently</h2>

  <p>The minuses first. Vocele has no guided course with a fixed lesson order, no Korean or Chinese, no Android or web app, and it isn't on the App Store yet, so there are no ratings to compare. It runs on iPhone with iOS 17 or later.</p>

  <h3>Conversation in three support modes</h3>

  <p>You talk with a named coach, Luna or Lorenzo, about whatever you choose, and the replies are spoken aloud. There's no lesson script to work through and finish. Three modes set how much help you get: full scaffolding when you need help building each sentence, corrections-only, and immersive. A beginner starts in the first, and an intermediate learner who's bored of drills goes straight to the third.</p>

  <h3>Corrections inside the sentence you said</h3>

  <p>When you get something wrong, Vocele highlights the wrong words inside your own bubble and puts the fix beneath, with rule chips. I picked that over rewriting your sentence in a separate card on purpose. A rewrite shows you the right version; the highlight shows you where yours broke. You can save the corrected sentence, and grammar rules are explained in your native language, with adaptive quizzes and a report of the sentences where you could have used the rule.</p>

  <h3>Words that come back</h3>

  <p>Long-press a word in chat, tap a coach chip, or save a correction, and it goes into your review. Vocabulary returns on a spaced-repetition schedule in six modes: flashcards, matching, typing, listening, pictures and fill-in-the-blank. Each day's plan is built from the previous day's errors, so what you got wrong on Tuesday is what you practice on Wednesday.</p>

  <h3>Pronunciation with a history</h3>

  <p>Vocele scores each word, diagnoses it down to the sound and keeps your score history over weeks, with phrase drills. You can check pronunciation on any spoken turn in a chat. My view: a single score is a verdict, and a trend is a coach. A lenient score that says "perfect" today tells you less than a strict one that's climbing week over week. The <a href="/blog/ai-accent-training">explainer on AI accent training</a> covers how per-word scoring works.</p>
